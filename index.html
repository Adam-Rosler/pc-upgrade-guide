Upgrading Adam's computer

Hi Mom! This is everything, one step at a time. You'll do Parts 1 and 2 at home (about 30 minutes in total), then a computer shop does Part 3. Adam helps from his laptop the whole way.

If you're ever unsure

Stop, take a photo of the screen or the computer with your phone, and text it to Adam. Nothing here breaks if you wait.

1

The software update

About 15 minutes. The old parts stay in for this one.

This teaches the computer about its new processor. It must happen first, while the old parts are still inside.

  1. Make sure the computer's screen, keyboard and mouse are plugged in and the screen is on.
  2. Plug the USB stick into any USB port on the back of the computer.
  3. Text Adam: USB is in. He'll put the update on it and restart the computer for you.
  4. The screen shows a menu with MSI at the top. Find the button that says M-FLASH (usually bottom-left) and click it. If it asks "Are you sure?", click Yes.
  5. It restarts and shows a list. Click the USB stick, then the file named E7A38AMS.BP4, then Yes.
  6. Leave it completely alone for 10 minutes. It finishes and restarts by itself.
    Don't turn it off, unplug it, or press anything, even if the screen goes black or it restarts on its own. That's normal.
  7. When it's back, text Adam: Update done.

2

The new memory

About 15 minutes. You'll swap 2 old sticks for 4 new ones.

Turn it off and open it

  1. Text Adam: Ready for memory. He turns the computer off for you.
  2. When the screen is dark and the lights are off, flip the switch on the back to O and unplug the power cord.
  3. Touch a bare metal part of the case for a second. This clears static electricity, which can damage the parts.
  4. Take out the 2 screws on the back edge of the side panel, slide the panel back and lift it off. Lay the computer on its side, open side up.

Swap the sticks

  1. Remove the 2 old sticks: push the clip at each end of the slot down, then pull the stick straight up. Put the old sticks in the eBay box.
  2. Fill all 4 slots with the new sticks. Each stick has a small notch in its gold edge. Line it up with the bump inside the slot; it only fits one way.
  3. Press firmly until both clips snap shut with a click. It takes more push than you'd expect. If a clip didn't close, the stick isn't in yet.
  4. Put the side panel back on, plug the cord in, flip the switch to I, and press the power button.
    The first start can show a black screen for 1 to 3 minutes while it gets used to the new memory. That's normal. Don't turn it off.
  5. Text Adam: It's on. He'll check from his laptop that it sees all the new memory.

3

At a computer shop

Bring the computer (no screen or keyboard needed) and 3 boxes.

Take the computer to a local computer repair shop with the processor box (AMD Ryzen 9), the fan box (Thermalright) and the graphics card box (GeForce RTX 5070 Ti). Show them this:

"Please install this Ryzen 9 processor with this Thermalright cooler, and swap the graphics card for this RTX 5070 Ti. The BIOS is already updated and the new memory is already in. Please check the power supply is at least 750 W and connect the card's power cable. If the power supply is too small, please tell me before changing it. Please give me back the old processor and graphics card."

Before you pay, ask them to:

  • turn it on once and make sure it starts
  • check the new fan spins
  • give back the old processor and the old graphics card (Adam can sell them)

Back home: plug it in, turn it on, and text Adam Back from the shop. He does everything else from his laptop.

Things never to do

  • Never unplug or switch off the computer during Part 1's update.
  • Never touch the gold edges of the memory sticks or the chips if you can help it. Hold them by the sides.
  • Never force anything. If a stick won't go in, turn it around: the notch only fits one way.
