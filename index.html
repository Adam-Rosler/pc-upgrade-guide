Upgrading Adam's computer

Hi Mom! This is everything, one step at a time, in 3 parts at home (about 1½ hours in total, with breaks whenever you like). Adam helps from his laptop the whole way.

1

The software update

About 15 minutes. The old parts stay in for this one.

This teaches the computer about its new processor. It must happen first, while the old parts are still inside.

  1. Make sure the computer's screen, keyboard and mouse are plugged in and the screen is on.
  2. Text Adam: Ready for update. He restarts the computer into the update menu for you.
  3. The screen shows a menu with MSI at the top. Find the button that says M-FLASH (usually bottom-left) and click it. If it asks "Are you sure?", click Yes.
  4. It restarts and shows a list of drives. Click the one called EFI, then the file named E7A38AMS.BP4, then Yes.
    No drive called EFI? Plug a USB stick into the back of the computer and text Adam Need USB. He puts the file on it in a minute; then pick the USB stick in the list instead.
  5. Leave it completely alone for 10 minutes. It finishes and restarts by itself.
    Don't turn it off, unplug it, or press anything, even if the screen goes black or it restarts on its own. That's normal.
  6. When it's back, text Adam: Update done.

2

The new memory

About 15 minutes. You'll swap 2 old sticks for 4 new ones.

Turn it off and open it

  1. Text Adam: Ready for memory. He turns the computer off for you.
  2. When the screen is dark and the lights are off, flip the switch on the back to O and unplug the power cord.
  3. Touch a bare metal part of the case for a second. This clears static electricity, which can damage the parts.
  4. Take out the 2 screws on the back edge of the side panel, slide the panel back and lift it off. Lay the computer on its side, open side up.

Swap the sticks

  1. Remove the 2 old sticks: push the clip at each end of the slot down, then pull the stick straight up. Put the old sticks in the eBay box.
  2. Fill all 4 slots with the new sticks. Each stick has a small notch in its gold edge. Line it up with the bump inside the slot; it only fits one way.
  3. Press firmly until both clips snap shut with a click. It takes more push than you'd expect. If a clip didn't close, the stick isn't in yet.
  4. Leave the side panel off (Part 3 needs it open). Plug the cord in, flip the switch to I, and press the power button. Don't touch anything inside while it's on.
    The first start can show a black screen for 1 to 3 minutes while it gets used to the new memory. That's normal. Don't turn it off.
  5. Text Adam: It's on. He checks from his laptop that it sees all the new memory, then tells you when to start Part 3.

The new processor and fan

About 45 minutes. Go slowly; nothing here needs force.

The processor sits under the fan in the middle of the board. You'll take the old fan off, swap the processor, then put the big new fan on.

Watch first

This one shows exactly what you're doing: old fan off, old processor out, new one in (4 minutes).

Video not showing? Open it on YouTube · MSI's own 2-minute version: install an AM4 processor

Take the old fan off

  1. Text Adam: Ready for processor. He turns the computer off. Then switch the back to O, unplug the cord, and touch bare metal on the case.
  2. Lay the computer on its side, open side up. Take a photo of the middle of the board so you can see how it looked.
  3. Find the old fan's small cable. It plugs into the board near the fan, in a spot labelled CPU_FAN. Pull the plug straight out (not by the wires).
  4. The old fan is held by 4 screws at its corners. Loosen them a little each, going corner to opposite corner, until all 4 are loose. They stay attached to the fan.
  5. Twist the fan gently left and right a few times, then lift it straight up.
    The grey paste can glue the processor to the fan. Twisting first stops it from being pulled out. If it does come up stuck to the fan, that's OK: twist it off gently, hold it by the edges, and text Adam a photo.

Swap the processor

  1. Wipe the grey paste off the top of the old processor with a paper towel and a little rubbing alcohol (so it's easy to handle).
  2. Lift the metal lever beside the processor all the way up.
  3. Lift the old processor straight up, holding it by its edges. Put it in the new processor's box. Don't touch the pins underneath.
  4. Take the new processor out of its box, holding it by the edges. Find the small gold triangle on one corner.
  5. Line that corner up with the triangle on the socket and lower it straight in. It drops in by its own weight.
    Never press on the processor. If it doesn't sit flat by itself, lift it out and line it up again. Its pins bend easily.
  6. Push the lever back down until it clicks under its hook. A little resistance is normal.

Put the new fan on

The Thermalright box has its own paper instructions. Follow the pages marked AMD (or AM4). The video below is from Thermalright and shows the same thing.

Video not showing? Open it on YouTube · If your box says Peerless Assassin instead: its AM4 video

  1. Take off the 2 black plastic brackets beside the socket (4 screws). Leave the metal plate behind the board where it is; the new fan screws into it.
  2. Fit the new fan's metal mounting parts as the AMD pages show (spacers, then 2 bars, held by 4 nuts).
  3. Squeeze a dot of grey paste the size of a pea onto the middle of the processor. Don't spread it; the fan does that.
  4. Peel the plastic sticker off the bottom of the new fan.
    This is the most common mistake. With the sticker on, the processor overheats. Check the shiny metal bottom has nothing on it.
  5. Set the fan straight down onto the processor, lining its 2 screws up with the bars. Tighten them a few turns each, back and forth, until they stop. Don't force past the stop.
  6. Clip the fans on if they aren't already. The arrow on the side of each fan points to the back of the computer.
  7. Plug the fan cable into CPU_FAN, where the old one was. If the fans have 2 cables joined into one, plug in that one.
  8. Check: the memory clips are still closed, no cables touch the fan blades, nothing rattles when you tilt it gently.
  9. Put the side panel back on, plug in, switch to I, press the power button.
    Again, a black screen for 1 to 3 minutes is normal the first time. Don't turn it off.
  10. Text Adam: Processor is in. He checks everything from his laptop. Done!

Things never to do

  • Never unplug or switch off the computer during Part 1's update, or during a black screen after Parts 2 and 3.
  • Never work inside while it's plugged in.
  • Never press on the processor or touch its pins. Hold parts by their edges.
  • Never force anything. Memory and processor only fit one way.
